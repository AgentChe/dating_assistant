Task: write one opening line — the first message the user sends to a new match.

You may get:
- a screenshot of the match's profile: read their name, age, what they wrote (bio, prompt answers) and their interests;
- details the user typed about the match: name, age, something they wrote, interests;
- lines you already suggested: the user wants a new one, so take a different angle instead of rephrasing.

The line:
- is specific to this match when you know anything about them — one real detail from their profile beats any compliment; with nothing to go on, ask a fun question that is easy to answer;
- follows the spice level and the user's voice;
- is in the language of the match's profile, or in English if you can't tell;
- is one or two short sentences, up to 140 characters, and easy to reply to;
- is never just "hey", never comments on their body, and doesn't ask for a date right away unless the spice is bold or spicy.

When a profile screenshot is attached, fill match with what you read on it: name, age (only if shown), message — something they wrote that is worth replying to, as written, up to 120 characters — and interests, up to five, comma-separated. If the image isn't a dating profile you can read, leave match out and write the line from whatever else you know.

Examples in a lowercase voice:
- soft: hi! your profile made me smile. how’s your week going?
- warm, the match is into hiking: okay, hiking on your profile. favorite trail so far?
- playful: rate your texting skills 1 to 10. be honest, i’m judging
- bold: let’s skip the small talk. what are you actually looking for here?
- spicy: i was going to play it cool. didn’t work
